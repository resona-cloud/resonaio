Resona — Case Studies

Here's What We've Built.

Every engagement follows the same four phases: Discovery, Architecture, Brand & Execution, and Systemize. Here's how that played out on real problems.

Different industries and different problems, handled with the same methodology. The results held up after we stepped back.

Consumer App · Family Tech
A Platform Built to Reconnect What Life Had Pulled Apart
Discovery Architecture Brand & Execution Systemize
The Problem
Families going through separation, distance, or instability often have no shared system: no structure, no guidance, no clear path forward. The founder knew what connection should look like. What was missing was a plan for building it, a brand that could carry the emotional weight of the mission, and a product roadmap that would hold up with real users. The idea was solid, and the product needed the structure to match.
What We Built
We started with architecture: the core user flows, the operating model, and the decision cycles that would keep the platform intuitive under pressure. The brand needed a name, a visual identity, and a voice that felt safe and structured without feeling clinical. After that came a phased product roadmap with clear milestones, a position that spoke directly to the people who needed it most, and an operating framework the founder could run without us. The outcome was a working platform with a clear position, a brand its users trusted, and a system the founder could keep building on.
Hardware · MedTech
A Product That Could Change Lives, If Anyone Could Find It
Discovery Architecture Brand & Execution Systemize
The Problem
3D-printed medical bracing could dramatically improve patient outcomes at a fraction of the traditional cost. The company had the product. What it lacked was the support around it: a clear plan for scaling production decisions, a brand that could earn trust in a high-stakes medical setting, and a path forward that said where to go next and how to hold their position once larger players took notice.
What We Built
We built the architecture first: operating frameworks for product decisions, supply chain logic, and feedback loops that would let them improve the product without losing consistency. The brand followed, with a visual language and positioning meant to earn trust in a clinical setting while staying approachable for the patients and providers it served. Then we mapped what came next: a launch strategy built on their defensible advantages, a pricing model that reflected the value they delivered, and a roadmap that gave them a sequence instead of a list of ideas. They left with a brand, a plan, and a clear direction for the product.
Retail · Legacy Business · Tennessee
Twelve Years of Trust and No Way to Sell Online
Discovery Architecture Brand & Execution Systemize
The Problem
Twelve years in business is an achievement, but the systems that got you there may not be built to scale. This salon had deep roots, a loyal client base, and a product line with real commercial potential. Then the shift to online sales exposed every gap at once: no e-commerce setup, no digital brand presence, and no launch plan for a market where the best product online beats the best product on the shelf. The business wasn't failing, but it had no system for keeping up as the market moved.
What We Built
We started with an honest audit of where the business really stood, setting aside assumptions to see how things actually ran. From there we rebuilt the launch strategy from the ground up: a digital brand presence that carried twelve years of credibility into a new channel, an e-commerce setup designed around their product line and how their customers buy, and a process overhaul that made online sales a core part of daily operations. They ended up with a system built to keep adapting as the market changes.
B2B · Sales Enablement
A Sales Methodology That Only Worked If You Were In the Room
Discovery Architecture Brand & Execution Systemize
The Problem
The methodology worked. Deals closed and reps trusted it. But it only lived as tribal knowledge and a slide deck that changed every time someone new presented it. There was no packaged way to train another company's sales force on it, and no polished way to put it in front of a prospective client. It was a real competitive edge with nothing built to sell it.
What We Built
We started with architecture, breaking the methodology into a structured, repeatable curriculum a sales team could be trained on. Next came a micro-brand of its own, separate from the parent company, built to carry the methodology as a standalone product. Then execution: 3D presentation assets and client engagement materials designed so the pitch itself showed the methodology in action instead of only describing it. The outcome was a sellable training product with its own identity, something a sales force could be trained on and hand to a client in the same breath, without the original team re-explaining it each time.
Travel & Hospitality
Profitable and Growing, but No One Could Say What They Were Buying
Discovery Architecture Brand & Execution Systemize
The Problem
The business itself was healthy. It ran, it made money, and it delivered real service. But everything it did lived as a loose bundle of services instead of a defined product. That works while the founder can hold the whole picture in their head. It stops working when growth means bringing in outsiders like investors, new customers, and new hires, none of whom can buy into a feeling.
What We Built
We started with architecture, mapping the service delivery model to find where the real product value lived, separate from the day-to-day operations around it. Then we packaged that value into something concrete: a defined, quantifiable product with clear boundaries, not an open-ended bundle of services. Brand and positioning followed, built on the same product definition so it read the same way to an investor, a customer, or a new hire. The product could now be evaluated on its own, without the founder in the room to explain it.
Consumer Goods · Specialty Coffee
Great Coffee, Lost on a Crowded Shelf
Discovery Architecture Brand & Execution Systemize
The Problem
The coffee wasn't the issue. Beans imported directly from East Africa gave the company a real, defensible quality advantage. What it lacked was a way to make that advantage visible in a local espresso market already crowded with competitors telling a similar story. With no strategy for entering that market and no brand to carry the story of where the coffee came from, a genuinely better product was getting lost on the shelf next to everyone else's.
What We Built
We started with a strategy for competing in a saturated local market: where the real openings were, and what would make this coffee worth choosing over an established competitor. Next came a visual identity and positioning built around the sourcing story, which was already true and didn't need inventing for marketing. Execution turned that strategy into the product experience itself, with packaging, presence, and positioning that made the origin and quality clear at a glance. The result was measurable market share growth, built on positioning that keeps working long after the initial push, unlike a one-time promotional bump.
